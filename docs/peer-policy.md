# Comparable-company policy and reconciliation

The student's pre-selection policy is preserved in `legacy/lab08-uipath-comps.md`. It favors recurring enterprise workflow/automation software with reasonably comparable growth, high gross margins and expansion economics; it excludes materially different business models and prices distorted by a signed takeover or financial distress. Preserve that chronology. This final extension adds an EV/revenue calculation; it does not pretend that new calculations existed before selection.

PEGA remains the one qualified numerical peer under the earlier student decision. Appian remains a business comparable but is excluded from P/E because its $0.02 annual EPS makes that reference economically fragile. Dynatrace was an uninvestigated AI suggestion, not an admitted peer. No new peer is silently selected to reach a preferred valuation.

The added EV multiple uses **EV/LTM recognized revenue**, not ARR or ACV. EV = contemporaneous market price x latest available basic shares + funded debt - unrestricted cash and marketable securities. Leases stay operating for both firms. No preferred/minority claim is identified in the selected balance sheets. This funded-debt-only market convention excludes nonfunded acquisition claims for both firms; the DCF instead deducts PATH's known $34.251m acquisition claim. Deducting that claim from the peer-implied target equity would lower the EV/revenue reference by about $0.07/share, to roughly $12.69. All liquid assets are deducted consistently in this market convention; unlike the DCF, no required-cash reserve is retained. Do not treat the two EV definitions as identical without that reconciliation.

Both prices are October 19 intraday snapshots: PATH $13.03 at 11:15 a.m. EDT and PEGA $34.37 at 11:25 a.m. EDT. The ten-minute mismatch is a limitation. Share counts and balance sheets are lagged and dated in the input file: PATH September 3 shares/July 31 financials; PEGA July 13 shares/June 30 financials. These are estimated market caps from latest available shares, not observed same-day share ledgers. LTM revenue uses annual + current H1 - prior H1 for both firms. Revenue periods differ by one month.

PEGA EV/LTM revenue is 3.046x versus PATH 3.130x. Applying the peer's ratio to PATH LTM revenue and reversing the same cash/debt bridge gives $12.75 per basic share. This is a single-peer reference, not a range or independent proof of fair value.

For the equity multiple, both firms use annual reported diluted EPS: PEGA $2.13 and PATH $0.52. PEGA P/E is 16.136x, implying PATH $8.39; PATH's own P/E is 25.058x. Neither normalized earnings nor non-GAAP EPS is mixed into this calculation. Different relative tax benefits remain a material contamination rather than cancelling automatically. The economic DCF uses basic shares and an SBC cash-cost proxy; do not describe the reported-EPS cross-check as using the identical share basis.

PEGA's newer slowdown, services mix and revenue recognition differences limit its usefulness. The June filing reports no cash borrowing and covenant compliance, but a comprehensive intervening-event screen through October 19 has not been completed here. The retained peer qualification is conditional on this limitation. The historical selection and source-check record are preserved, including conflicting AI EPS claims rejected against company releases.

No control transaction is used as valuation evidence. UiPath's own acquisitions inform its cash/claims bridge; they are not comparable-company transaction multiples. A defensible transaction multiple would require target economics, announcement date, control premium, synergy context and consistent EV definitions. Omitting an unqualified transaction is preferable to inventing one.

The methods are not averaged. Sales multiples roughly support the market while the operating DCF does not. The committee must decide whether stronger profitability than the analyst's cases is credible. This disagreement is a research question, not a calculation to hide.
